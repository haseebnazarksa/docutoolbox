What is "ideal body weight"?

Ideal body weight (IBW) formulas were originally developed for medical purposes, like calculating drug dosages, rather than as a fitness or aesthetic target. There isn't one universally agreed formula, several were developed by different researchers over the decades, which is why this calculator shows results from four commonly referenced formulas side by side instead of presenting a single number as definitive.

How to use this calculator

  1. Select your sex and enter your height.
  2. Click Calculate to see estimates from all four formulas plus their average.

The four formulas

  • Devine (1974) - originally created for medication dosing, now the most widely cited formula
  • Robinson (1983) - a refinement of the Devine formula using updated population data
  • Miller (1983) - another Devine-based refinement, tends to give slightly lower values
  • Hamwi (1964) - an older formula, originally developed for quick clinical use
These formulas are decades-old estimates based on height alone, they don't account for muscle mass, body composition, frame size, or individual health factors, and the different formulas often disagree with each other by several kilograms. Treat this as a rough historical reference rather than a personal target, and talk to a doctor or dietitian about what a healthy weight looks like for you specifically.

Frequently asked questions

Why do the four formulas give different results?

Each was developed independently using different population data and assumptions, decades apart, disagreement between them is expected and is exactly why no single formula should be treated as a precise, personal target.

Should I try to hit this exact weight?

Not necessarily, these formulas don't account for muscle mass, bone structure, or individual body composition, a healthy weight range is highly individual, this tool is best used as general context rather than a strict goal.
