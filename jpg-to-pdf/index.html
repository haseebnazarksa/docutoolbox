What is the JPG / Image to PDF tool?

This tool combines one or more images — JPG, PNG, or WEBP — into a single PDF document, with each image becoming its own page. It's built for turning scanned photos, screenshots, or picture-based documents into a proper, shareable PDF file.

Everything happens locally in your browser — your images are never uploaded to a server, so it's fast and private even for personal photos or sensitive scans.

How to use it

  1. Drop your images onto the upload area, or click it to browse your device — select as many as you need at once.
  2. Choose your page settings: page size (fit-to-image, or a standard A4/Letter page), and for A4/Letter, the orientation and margin.
  3. Drag any image thumbnail to change its order — the numbers update live to show the final page order.
  4. Click the × on any image to remove it before creating the PDF.
  5. Need more images? Click "+ Add more images" to add additional files to the set.
  6. Click Create PDF and download the result.

Choosing a page size

Fit to image makes each PDF page exactly the same size and shape as its source image — no white borders, no cropping, just the image itself as a page. This is the best choice when your images are already the right shape (like a scanned photo) and you want the PDF to preserve that exactly.

A4 and Letter place each image centered on a standard, fixed page size, scaling it down to fit if needed. This is useful when you want a document that prints consistently and matches standard paper sizes, especially if you're mixing images of different shapes and want them to look uniform.

Orientation and margins

When using A4 or Letter page sizes, you can choose portrait (taller than wide, the default for most documents) or landscape (wider than tall, useful for wide photos or panoramas). The margin setting controls how much blank space surrounds the image on the page — "None" fills the page edge-to-edge, while "Small" or "Large" leaves a visible white border, similar to a printed photo mat.

How it works technically

Each image is placed onto its own PDF page according to your chosen size and orientation settings, preserving the original image quality — images are embedded directly into the PDF rather than being re-compressed or degraded in the process.

Common uses

  • Turning a batch of scanned document photos into one shareable PDF
  • Combining screenshots into a single file for a report or bug submission
  • Creating a simple photo book or portfolio PDF from a set of images
  • Converting a phone-scanned receipt or ID photo into a PDF for form submissions
  • Assembling multiple whiteboard or notes photos into one organized document

File size and privacy

Because everything happens on your device, there's no upload wait and no privacy risk. A large number of high-resolution images may take a little longer to process, depending on your device's available memory, since everything runs locally rather than on a remote server.

Frequently asked questions

Can I mix JPG, PNG, and WEBP images in the same PDF?

Yes — you can upload any combination of these formats, and they'll all be combined into the same PDF, each becoming its own page.

Will converting reduce my image quality?

No — your original image data is embedded into the PDF without additional lossy recompression, so quality is preserved.

Can I change the order after adding images?

Yes — simply drag any image thumbnail to a new position in the grid at any time before creating the PDF.

Is there a limit to how many images I can combine?

There's no hard limit built into the tool, though a very large number of high-resolution images will naturally take longer to process since everything runs on your device.
