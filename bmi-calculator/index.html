What is BMI?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a simple calculation using your height and weight, used as a general screening tool to categorize weight ranges that may lead to health problems. It's calculated by dividing weight by the square of height, and is the same measurement doctors, the WHO, and the CDC reference when discussing weight-related health risk at a population level.

How to use this calculator

  1. Choose Metric or Imperial units.
  2. Enter your weight and height.
  3. Click Calculate BMI to see your result, category, and healthy weight range on the visual gauge.

BMI categories

  • Below 18.5 - Underweight
  • 18.5 - 24.9 - Healthy weight
  • 25 - 29.9 - Overweight
  • 30 - 34.9 - Obesity (Class 1)
  • 35 and above - Obesity (Class 2 and above)

The BMI formula

Metric: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². Imperial: BMI = (weight (lb) ÷ height (in)²) × 703. This calculator applies the same formula used by the CDC and WHO for adults.

Limitations of BMI

BMI is a useful screening tool but doesn't directly measure body fat, and doesn't account for muscle mass, bone density, age, or sex. A very muscular person can have a high BMI without excess body fat, while someone with a "normal" BMI could still carry unhealthy levels of body fat. BMI is best used as one data point alongside other health measurements, not a standalone diagnosis.

This tool provides a general estimate for informational purposes and is not medical advice. Talk to a doctor or healthcare provider about what a healthy weight looks like for you specifically.

Frequently asked questions

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

Not always, athletes and people with high muscle mass often score higher on BMI despite having low body fat, since BMI can't distinguish between muscle and fat.

Does BMI work the same for children?

No, children and teens are assessed using age- and sex-specific percentile charts rather than the fixed adult categories used here, this calculator is intended for adults.
