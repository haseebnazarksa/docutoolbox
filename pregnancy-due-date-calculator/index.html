How is a due date calculated?

The most common method, called Naegele's Rule, estimates a due date as 280 days (40 weeks) from the first day of your last menstrual period, assuming a typical 28-day cycle. If you know your conception date instead, the due date is estimated as 266 days (38 weeks) from that date. This calculator adjusts automatically for cycle lengths other than 28 days when using the last period method.

How to use this calculator

  1. Choose whether to calculate from your last period or a known conception date.
  2. Enter the date, and your average cycle length if using the last period method.
  3. Click Calculate due date to see your estimated due date, current week, and trimester.

Understanding the trimesters

  • First trimester - weeks 1 through 13
  • Second trimester - weeks 14 through 27
  • Third trimester - weeks 28 through 40
This calculator provides a general estimate only, roughly 1 in 20 births occur on the exact calculated due date, with most happening within a couple weeks before or after. This is not medical advice, always confirm dates and monitor pregnancy progress with a doctor or midwife, who may use ultrasound measurements for a more precise estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my doctor's due date differ slightly from this calculator?

Doctors often adjust the estimate based on an early ultrasound, which measures the fetus directly and can be more accurate than a date-based calculation alone, especially if your cycle is irregular.

What if I don't know my exact last period date?

If you know your conception date (for example, from fertility tracking or IVF), use the conception date method instead for a more direct estimate.
