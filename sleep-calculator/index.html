What is the Sleep Calculator?

Sleep happens in cycles lasting roughly 90 minutes each, moving through light sleep, deep sleep, and REM sleep. Waking up at the end of a full cycle, rather than in the middle of one, tends to feel less groggy than waking mid-cycle, even if it means slightly less total sleep.

How to use this calculator

  1. Choose whether you want to plan around a wake-up time or a bedtime.
  2. Enter the known time.
  3. The calculator shows matching times in 90-minute increments, so you can wake at the end of a full sleep cycle.

Why cycles matter

Most adults go through 4 to 6 sleep cycles per night. Rather than just aiming for "8 hours," timing your sleep in full cycles can make it easier to wake up feeling rested, since you're less likely to be pulled out of deep sleep by an alarm.

This calculator is a general planning guide based on an average 90-minute cycle length, actual cycle length varies somewhat from person to person and night to night. This is not medical advice, if you have ongoing sleep difficulties, talk to a doctor.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it typically take to fall asleep?

This calculator assumes roughly 15 minutes to fall asleep after getting into bed, which is added on top of the cycle calculation when working backward from a wake-up time.

Is more sleep always better?

Not necessarily in terms of how rested you feel, waking mid-cycle (even after more total hours) often feels worse than waking at the end of a cycle with slightly less total sleep, timing can matter as much as duration.
