How does a calorie deficit work?

Weight loss happens when you consistently eat fewer calories than your body burns (your maintenance calories, or TDEE), forcing it to draw on stored energy. This calculator estimates your maintenance calories using the Mifflin-St Jeor formula, then subtracts a deficit sized to your chosen pace, since 1kg (2.2lb) of body fat represents roughly 7,700 calories.

How to use this calculator

  1. Enter your sex, age, weight, height, and activity level.
  2. Choose a goal pace, slower paces are generally easier to sustain and more likely to preserve muscle mass.
  3. Click Calculate to see your maintenance calories and daily target.

Why gradual is generally better

Slower, moderate deficits (around 0.5-1 lb per week) are widely associated with better long-term adherence and a lower risk of muscle loss compared to aggressive, very low-calorie approaches. Rapid weight loss is also more likely to be regained. This calculator intentionally caps its options at a moderate range rather than offering extreme deficits.

This tool provides a general estimate for informational purposes and is not medical or nutritional advice. It is not intended for anyone with a history of disordered eating, and calorie targets below general safe minimums should only be followed under medical supervision. Talk to a doctor or registered dietitian before starting a weight loss plan.

Frequently asked questions

Why did I get a warning about my calorie target?

This appears if the calculated target falls below commonly cited safe minimums (roughly 1,500 kcal for men, 1,200 kcal for women), very low intakes can be difficult to sustain nutritionally and should be supervised by a healthcare professional.

Does the deficit account for exercise calories separately?

No, your selected activity level already factors typical exercise into your maintenance calories, if you do a workout well above your usual activity level, your actual deficit that day may be larger than planned.
