What are BMR and TDEE?

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest just to keep basic functions running, breathing, circulation, cell production. TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) adds your activity level on top of BMR, giving a full picture of how many calories you burn in an average day, including exercise and daily movement.

Which formula should I use?

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation, developed in 1990, is generally considered more accurate for most modern populations and is the formula most commonly recommended by dietitians today. The Harris-Benedict equation, originally published in 1919 and revised in 1984, is an older alternative still widely referenced, results are typically very close between the two.

Activity level guide

  • Sedentary - desk job, little to no structured exercise
  • Light - light exercise or sports 1-3 days a week
  • Moderate - moderate exercise or sports 3-5 days a week
  • Active - hard exercise or sports 6-7 days a week
  • Very active - hard daily exercise plus a physically demanding job
This tool provides general estimates for informational purposes and is not medical or nutritional advice. Individual calorie needs vary, consult a doctor or registered dietitian before making significant changes to your diet.

Frequently asked questions

Should I eat exactly my TDEE number every day?

TDEE is a starting estimate, not an exact science, actual needs vary day to day and person to person. Many people track results over a few weeks and adjust intake based on how their weight actually changes.

How big should a calorie deficit or surplus be?

A moderate deficit of around 500 calories per day below TDEE is a commonly used guideline for gradual weight loss (roughly 0.5kg/1lb per week), and the reverse for weight gain, though individual needs and goals vary.
