What is a slug?

A slug is the URL-friendly version of a title or phrase, using only lowercase letters, numbers, and a separator (usually a hyphen) in place of spaces and punctuation. For example, "10 Best Tips for Beginners!" becomes 10-best-tips-for-beginners. Slugs are used in the URL of blog posts, product pages, and articles across virtually every content management system.

How to use it

  1. Type or paste your title into the box.
  2. The slug updates live below as you type.
  3. Choose a hyphen or underscore separator, and whether to force lowercase.
  4. Click Copy to copy the generated slug.

Why slugs matter for URLs

  • Readability - a clean slug like /best-coffee-shops is easier to read and remember than a random ID
  • SEO - search engines use words in a URL as a relevance signal, a descriptive slug can help with search rankings
  • Compatibility - removing spaces and special characters avoids URL encoding issues and broken links

Common uses

  • Generating a URL slug for a new blog post or article
  • Creating a clean file name from a document title
  • Building product page URLs for an online store
  • Converting a heading into an anchor link ID for a page

Frequently asked questions

What happens to special characters and accents?

Special characters and punctuation are removed entirely, and accented letters are converted to their closest plain equivalent (like "é" becoming "e") to keep the slug URL-safe.

Should I use a hyphen or underscore?

Hyphens are the standard convention and generally recommended, most major search engines and CMS platforms treat hyphens as word separators for SEO purposes, while underscores are sometimes treated as a single connected word instead.
